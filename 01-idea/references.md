
# ReWear – Design References

## 1. Competitors

### 1. Whering
Link: https://www.whering.co/

**What to take:** I like the visual presentation of clothing items and the outfit creation feature. The colorful interface makes wardrobe browsing more engaging.

**What to avoid:** I want to avoid too many features competing for the user's attention. ReWear should make discovering an outfit simple and quick.

### 2. Acloset
Link: https://www.acloset.app/

**What to take:** I like the clear organization of the wardrobe and the quick access to features such as adding clothes and creating outfits.

**What to avoid:** I want to avoid making the home screen too crowded with AI tools and extra features.

### 3. Stylebook
Link: https://stylebookapp.com/

**What to take:** I like the organized clothing categories and the calendar for planning outfits. These features make wardrobe management practical.

**What to avoid:** I want to avoid a complicated interface with too many menus and options.

### 4. Indyx
Link: https://www.myindyx.com/

**What to take:** I like the elegant typography, large fashion photography and calm color palette. The visual style feels modern and editorial.

**What to avoid:** I want to avoid using too much promotional content and making the main wardrobe features difficult to find.

## 2. Design Patterns


### Pattern 5 – Outfit Planning (Calendar)
**Source:** https://www.whering.co/

**What to take:** I like how users can view their planned outfits on specific dates. This can help ReWear users organize their outfits and decide what to wear in advance.

**What to avoid:** I would avoid making the calendar difficult to navigate or requiring too many steps to add an outfit.

### Pattern 6 – Digital Wardrobe
https://www.whering.co/

**What to take:** I like how uploaded clothing items are displayed in a personal digital wardrobe. The search and filter options make it easier for users to find specific clothes.

**What to avoid:** I would avoid a crowded layout or small clothing images that make it difficult to recognize individual items.

### Pattern 7 – Community Outfit Inspiration
https://www.whering.co/

**What to take:** I like how users can discover outfit combinations created by other people. This can inspire ReWear users to create new outfits using clothes they already own.

**What to avoid:** I would avoid making social features more important than the main purpose of the app, which is creating outfits from the user's own wardrobe.



## 3. Visual References

### Visual Reference 1 – Digital Outfit Styling

**Source:** https://whering.co.uk/outfit-maker-apps

**What to take:** I like how individual clothing items are displayed and combined to create complete outfits. I want to use a similar visual approach in ReWear to help users discover new outfit combinations using clothes they already own.

**What to avoid:** I would avoid overcrowding the styling screen with too many options or unnecessary elements that make outfit creation confusing.

### Visual Reference 2 – Fashion Color Palette (Alpine Oat)

**Source:** https://www.pinterest.com/search/pins/?q=Alpine%20Oat%20Pinterest%20Palette%202025

**What to take:** I like the warm beige, cream, and neutral tones used in this fashion moodboard. I want to use a similar color palette to create a calm, elegant, and modern visual identity for ReWear.

**What to avoid:** I would avoid using too many similar light colors without enough contrast, especially for text and important buttons.

### Visual Reference 3 – Clothing Photography

**Source:** https://www.pexels.com/photo/set-of-clothes-on-white-fabric-14577586/?utm_source=chatgpt.com

**What to take:** I like how the coat, sweater, jeans, and bag are arranged together in a clean flat-lay composition. I want to use a similar photography style to present clothing items and outfit combinations in ReWear.

**What to avoid:** I would avoid distracting backgrounds, inconsistent lighting, or overlapping clothing items that make individual pieces difficult to recognize.
